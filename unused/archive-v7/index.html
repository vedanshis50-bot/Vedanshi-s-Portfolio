I never really switched careers.

I just found a place where asking "Why?" became part of the job. This is the path most of my projects take.

  1. ConversationReal people, real insights
  2. NotebookIdeas, scribbles and chaos
  3. WhiteboardConnecting ideas before pixels
  4. Find patternsI have killed more ideas
  5. ResearchLearning what doesn't work

I didn't switch disciplines. I changed the scale.

Architecture taught me to think about space, structure and constraints. Product design taught me to think about behaviour, systems and people. The medium changed. The curiosity didn't. Scroll, and watch a floor plan become a product screen.

I thought these were the problems. I was wrong.

Every project began with a problem everyone thought they understood. The real work started when I questioned it.

So what actually deserves to be designed?

That's a more useful question than whether AI replaces designers. When anyone can generate a passable screen in seconds, the value shifts upstream, to the decisions that were never really about pixels.

  • Framing the right problem
  • Understanding real constraints
  • Making trade-offs on purpose
  • Deciding what not to build
  • Designing coherent systems
  • Designing behaviour, not just pixels

−A cleaner interface makes a product better.

At Leadrat I spent weeks making the screens cleaner. A salesperson looked at it and said he still didn't know what to do first.

+Nicer is not better.

−Following the brief is the actual job.

I used to treat a brief as a task. If I don't check it first, I just solve the wrong problem neatly.

+The brief is a guess.

−AI is a way to produce faster.

Now I use it to poke holes in my own ideas early, so I don't fall in love with the first one.

+AI is for arguing, not for answers.
